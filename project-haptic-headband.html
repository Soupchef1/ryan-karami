GameSense: Accessible Gaming Headband

TeamTeam of 4: William Schotte, Sumedh Kothari, Chase Twyman and me
ToolsESP32, C++, Python, WASAPI, Altium
LinksGitHub repository · Devpost

A wearable headband that lets deaf and hard-of-hearing gamers feel where sounds are coming from. GameSense captures a game's 7.1 surround audio, works out the direction and intensity of sounds like footsteps and gunfire in real time, and plays them back as vibrations around the player's head. Head-tilt sensing adds hands-free movement control. It won the Tech(Hack) hackathon.

The Problem

In competitive games, sound is information. Footsteps, gunfire and other spatial audio tell you where threats are, and deaf and hard-of-hearing players miss all of it. We wanted to turn that audio into something players could feel instead of hear.

The design had to work with real games, respond fast enough to be useful in play, and be comfortable enough to wear for a whole session. We also wanted to help players with limited hand or arm mobility, which led to adding head-tilt controls.

The Process

Capturing game audio

We set the game to native 7.1 surround output and captured the stream at the operating-system level with Windows' WASAPI loopback. This gave us separate channels for each direction without touching the game itself, which mattered because hooking into a game directly risks setting off its anti-cheat system.

Turning sound into direction

A Python program processes the captured audio in real time to estimate where each sound is coming from and how loud it is. It then maps that to motor outputs, so a footstep behind you on the left becomes a vibration at the back left of your head.

The headband

Eight vibration motors sit around a lightweight headband, driven by an ESP32 running embedded C++ firmware. An IMU tracks head tilt, so small head movements can control movement in game. We built and tested the circuit on a breadboard first, then designed an enclosure for the electronics.

The Outcome

We finished a working prototype within the hackathon: a light, comfortable headband that players stop noticing after a few minutes, with directional vibration that makes in-game audio feel immersive. GameSense won the Tech(Hack) hackathon.

Next steps would be support for macOS, Linux and consoles such as PlayStation, customizable feedback profiles, and an open-source release.

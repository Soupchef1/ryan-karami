    Headshot

    As a middle schooler, I loved aviation. I competed in model glider competitions and spent hours working on gliders in my dad's woodshop. Going into high school, I was sure I wanted to pursue aerospace engineering, until I took AP Physics C and found I enjoyed circuits and electromagnetics far more than mechanics. Senior year, I built my own computer from old parts and really fell in love with electronic hardware.

    Freshman year, I joined SiliconJackets to learn how computer chips are designed. The onboarding project, a 64-bit adder with memory interfacing, was honestly simple in hindsight, but at the time it was new and challenging. That project is what convinced me that digital chip design is what I want to pursue through undergrad and as a career.

    This past summer, I worked with some friends to build our own CPU, and I learned a lot about the basics of computer architecture and hardware-software co-design. At SiliconJackets, my first project was an SPI master module for a larger RISC-V processor, where I learned about serial communication, FIFOs, and control logic.

    Taking a signal processing class in Fall 2026 hooked me on digital signal processing and where it meets FPGA development.

    Going forward, I plan to keep learning about computer architecture and branch into GPUs and the AI/ML accelerators that will be essential in the future. I also want to explore signal processing applications and mixed-signal design, especially the analog-to-digital interface on modern SoCs.
